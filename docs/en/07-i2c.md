## Core ideas

**In one sentence: I2C connects several devices with two lines, SCL (the clock) and SDA (the data); each device can only pull a line low, and when everyone lets go a pull-up resistor brings the line back high. The master sends START and an address, the device with that address pulls SDA low on the 9th clock to acknowledge, and only then does data move.**

### An analogy first

A rope hangs from the classroom ceiling, and a spring at the top keeps pulling it up. Anyone in the room can reach up and pull the rope down, but nobody can push it up. While one person holds it, the rope stays down; only when everyone lets go does the spring pull it back.

The teacher (the master) beats time on a second rope and calls out a student number (the address). The student with that number gives the rope a tug on the next beat to say "here" (an acknowledge). If nobody tugs on that beat, that number is not in the room.

The analogy stops here. On a real bus the spring takes time to pull the line back: the larger the pull-up resistor and the capacitance on the line, the slower the voltage rises, and past some beat rate it cannot keep up. That is the rise time worked out in section 1. The number is not spoken either: it is a string of binary bits, one bit per beat of the clock line.

### 1. Electrical behavior: open drain, pull-ups, and wired-AND

Open drain is an output style where the pin has only a transistor to ground: writing 0 turns it on and pulls the line low; writing 1 turns it off and leaves the pin floating, with nothing pushing it high (RM0008 Rev 21 section 9.1.8). The high level comes entirely from the pull-up resistor: one end goes to the supply, the other to the signal line, and when nobody pulls the line low it holds the line high.

When many devices' open-drain pins share one line, you get a wired-AND: if any device pulls low, the whole line is low; the line is high only when every device lets go. I2C's acknowledge, clock synchronization and multi-master arbitration all rest on this (section 5).

| Property | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Pin type | Open drain; SDA and SCL both need pull-ups |
| Wired-AND | Low if any device pulls low; high only when all let go |
| Speed grades | Standard mode up to 100 kHz, fast mode up to 400 kHz (RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.1) |
| Multi-master, multi-slave | One bus can carry several masters and several slaves; slaves are told apart by address |

The table holds the four electrical rules of I2C; the later sections all follow from them.

Two points to remember on the F1:

- The two hardware I2C pins must be configured as alternate-function open-drain (RM0008 Rev 21 Table 27). In alternate-function mode and in output mode the chip's internal pull-up and pull-down resistors are disconnected (sections 9.1.8 and 9.1.9), so hardware I2C cannot use the on-chip pull-ups and the external pull-ups must be fitted.
- Even if you bit-bang and switch the pins to input with pull-up, the on-chip pull-up is 30–50 kΩ (DS5319 Rev 20 Table 36). That is too weak for I2C, as the numbers below show.

**Choosing the pull-up.** The resistor has an upper limit and a lower limit.

The upper limit comes from the rise time. The rise time tr is how long the line takes to go from 0.3 VDD to 0.7 VDD; those are the measurement points given in note 1 of DS5319 Rev 20 Figure 32. The pull-up Rp charges the bus capacitance Cb along an RC curve, and going from 0.3 VDD to 0.7 VDD takes exactly 0.8473 × Rp × Cb:

```text
tr      = 0.8473 × Rp × Cb               0.8473 = ln(0.7 / 0.3)
Rp(max) = tr(max) / (0.8473 × Cb)
tr(max): 1000 ns in standard mode, 300 ns in fast mode (DS5319 Rev 20 Table 41)
```

The lower limit comes from the sink current. When a device pulls low, current flows from the supply through Rp into its pin. The I2C specification requires a device sinking 3 mA to hold the line below 0.4 V (NXP's I2C specification UM10204, not in this book's source list), so:

```text
Rp(min) = (VDD − 0.4 V) / 3 mA = (3.3 − 0.4) / 0.003 ≈ 967 Ω
```

Put a few bus capacitances into the upper-limit formula:

| Bus capacitance Cb | Standard mode Rp(max) | Fast mode Rp(max) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 pF | 23.6 kΩ | 7.08 kΩ |
| 100 pF | 11.8 kΩ | 3.54 kΩ |
| 200 pF | 5.90 kΩ | 1.77 kΩ |
| 400 pF | 2.95 kΩ | 0.885 kΩ |

To use it, estimate the bus capacitance, look up the maximum for your speed grade, and pick a resistor no larger than that and no smaller than 967 Ω. 400 pF is the largest bus capacitance Table 41 allows; on that row the fast-mode limit of 0.885 kΩ is already below 967 Ω, which means a bus loaded to 400 pF cannot run 400 kHz with a single resistor.

The bus capacitance is the pin capacitance of every device plus the wiring. Each STM32 pin is 5 pF typical (DS5319 Rev 20 Table 36); a breadboard with one or two modules and a dozen or so centimetres of wire usually totals a few tens of picofarads (rule of thumb). DS5319 Table 42 uses a 4.7 kΩ external pull-up. By the formula above, in fast mode it can drive at most 300 ns ÷ (0.8473 × 4.7 kΩ) ≈ 75 pF, so 4.7 kΩ suits short wiring; with longer lines or more devices, switch to a smaller resistor such as 2.2 kΩ. Take the on-chip pull-up at its typical 40 kΩ: even on a 50 pF bus, 0.8473 × 40 kΩ × 50 pF ≈ 1.7 µs, which already misses the 1000 ns of standard mode.

### 2. Timing and the protocol: START, address, ACK, STOP

| Signal | Condition | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| START (S) | SDA falls while SCL is high | A transfer begins; the bus is busy |
| STOP (P) | SDA rises while SCL is high | A transfer ends; the bus is free |
| Data bit | SDA changes while SCL is low and holds still while SCL is high | The receiver reads the bit while SCL is high |
| ACK | On the 9th clock the receiver pulls SDA low | Received |
| NACK | On the 9th clock SDA stays high | No device answered, or the master has read its last byte and wants no more |

When you read the table, notice who sends the ACK: when the master writes, the slave acknowledges; when the master reads, the master acknowledges. After the last byte the master deliberately answers NACK to tell the slave to stop sending. The START and STOP level definitions come from the I2C specification UM10204; RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.3.1 states that data and addresses travel as 8-bit bytes, most significant bit first (MSB first), and that each byte is followed by a 9th clock on which the receiver sends the acknowledge bit.

Writing one command to the OLED looks like this on the wire (S is START, A is ACK, P is STOP):

```text
S | 0111100 0 | A | 0000 0000 | A | 1010 1110 | A | P
  | address 0x3C + write = 0x78  | control byte 0x00 | command 0xAE (display off)
```

Each byte takes 8 clocks, plus 1 clock for the acknowledge: 9 clocks. In fast mode at 400 kHz one clock is 2.5 µs, so a byte with its acknowledge takes 22.5 µs.

Compared with the UART: the UART sends the low bit first (chapter 06) and I2C sends the high bit first, so the same 0xA5 goes out in opposite bit order on the two buses.

DS5319 Rev 20 Table 41 sets the minimum durations of the clock and of the start and stop conditions. The table below picks the rows you use most, for checking against a logic analyzer:

| Parameter | Standard mode | Fast mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SCL low time tw(SCLL), min | 4.7 µs | 1.3 µs |
| SCL high time tw(SCLH), min | 4.0 µs | 0.6 µs |
| START hold th(STA), min | 4.0 µs | 0.6 µs |
| STOP setup tsu(STO), min | 4.0 µs | 0.6 µs |
| Bus free time from STOP to the next START tw(STO:STA), min | 4.7 µs | 1.3 µs |
| Rise time tr, max | 1000 ns | 300 ns |
| Bus capacitance per line Cb, max | 400 pF | 400 pF |

To use it: if the measured low and high times are no shorter than the minimums in a column and the rise time is no longer than the maximum, the bus meets that grade. Note 1 of Table 41 says these values are guaranteed by design, not tested in production.

### 3. Addresses: 7-bit, the 8-bit form, and 0x78

I2C addresses come in 7-bit and 10-bit forms; 7-bit is the common one. In 7-bit addressing, the first byte after START has two parts: the upper 7 bits are the address and the lowest bit is the read/write bit R/W, where 0 means the master will write and 1 means it will read (RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.3.3).

Taking 0x3C apart:

```text
The 7 bits of 0x3C:              011 1100
Shift left 1, R/W in the LSB:    0111 1000 = 0x78 (write)
                                 0111 1001 = 0x79 (read)
```

Device datasheets and example code use both forms: some write the 7-bit address 0x3C, others write the write byte 0x78 directly. HAL's `DevAddress` parameter wants the shifted value, `0x3C << 1`. If you pass the scanner's 0x3C as it is, the first byte on the wire is 0x3C, which is 7-bit address 0x1E; no device acknowledges and HAL reports an error.

| Device | 7-bit address | HAL DevAddress | How the address changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SSD1306 OLED | 0x3C | 0x78 | 0x3D with the SA0 pin high |
| AT24C02 EEPROM | 0x50 | 0xA0 | Pins A2, A1, A0 select 0x50–0x57 |
| MPU6050 | 0x68 | 0xD0 | 0x69 with the AD0 pin high |
| PCF8574 | 0x20–0x27 | 0x40–0x4E | Pins A2, A1, A0 select it |

Read the second column to confirm the 7-bit address, then the last column: the address of a given part often depends on how a few pins are tied, and jumpers or solder pads on the back of a module change it. These addresses come from each device's datasheet, which is not in this book's source list; trust the datasheet of the part in your hand. In the 7-bit address space, 0x00–0x07 and 0x78–0x7F are reserved by the I2C specification, so the scan skips them. Those are 7-bit addresses: the OLED's 0x78 is the shifted write byte, and its 7-bit address 0x3C is outside the reserved ranges.

### 4. Repeated start: the standard way to read a register

Sensors and EEPROMs have many registers or memory cells inside, and before reading you have to say which one. The order is:

```text
S | device addr + W | A | register addr | A | Sr | device addr + R | A | data | NACK | P
       0x78                                             0x79
```

The first half is a write that only writes a register address; instead of sending STOP the master immediately sends another START, the repeated start; the second half is a read. On the F1's I2C peripheral in master mode, setting the START bit again produces a repeated start at the end of the current byte (RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.3.3).

If you call `HAL_I2C_Master_Transmit` to write the register address and then `HAL_I2C_Master_Receive` to read, an extra STOP and a new START appear in between. Some devices require a repeated start there and throw away the register address they just received when they see a STOP; on a multi-master bus the bus is free after the STOP, and another master can get in. Whether a given device cares is in its datasheet. Doing it in one `Mem_Read` call avoids both cases.

Rule of thumb: **read registers with Mem_Read; the middle is Sr, not STOP and then START.**

### 5. Clock synchronization, arbitration, and clock stretching

All three follow directly from the wired-AND.

| Mechanism | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Clock synchronization | SCL is wired-AND too; whoever holds SCL low longest sets how long the low phase lasts |
| Arbitration | When two masters send at once, each reads SDA back while sending; the one that sends 1 but reads 0 knows someone else is sending 0 and drops out |
| Clock stretching | A slave that cannot keep up holds SCL low, and the master has to wait until it lets go |

A board with a single STM32 as master never needs arbitration, while clock stretching comes up often, so this table is more useful read from the bottom up. When the F1's I2C is master, after releasing SCL it waits the time set in TRISE and then checks SCL; if SCL is still low it concludes that a slave is stretching, and the high-level counter stops and waits (RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.3.3). When arbitration is lost, the ARLO bit in SR1 is set and the peripheral drops back to slave mode by itself (section 26.3.4). The NOSTRETCH bit in CR1 only controls whether the STM32 stretches when it is itself a slave; it has nothing to do with master mode (section 26.6.1).

### 6. Hardware I2C speed: FREQ, CCR, and TRISE

The F1's I2C peripheral counts the SCL high and low phases with the APB1 clock PCLK1, and three registers set it up (RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.6):

- FREQ[5:0] in I2C_CR2: tells the peripheral PCLK1 in MHz; write 36 here. The peripheral uses it to produce setup and hold times that meet the specification; the minimum is 2 MHz (section 26.6.2).
- I2C_CCR (offset 0x1C): bit 15 is F/S, 0 for standard mode and 1 for fast mode; bit 14 is DUTY; bits 11:0 are the CCR field (section 26.6.8).
- I2C_TRISE (offset 0x20): the maximum SCL rise time expressed in PCLK1 periods, plus 1 (section 26.6.9).

The CCR formulas are below, with TPCLK1 = 1 / 36 MHz ≈ 27.8 ns:

```text
Standard mode:        Thigh = CCR × TPCLK1        Tlow = CCR × TPCLK1
Fast mode, DUTY = 0:  Thigh = CCR × TPCLK1        Tlow = 2 × CCR × TPCLK1
Fast mode, DUTY = 1:  Thigh = 9 × CCR × TPCLK1    Tlow = 16 × CCR × TPCLK1
```

With 36 MHz:

| Target | Mode | CCR field | I2C_CCR | High / low | TRISE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 kHz | Standard, F/S = 0 | 36e6 / (2 × 100e3) = 180 | 0x00B4 | 5 µs / 5 µs | 1000 ns × 36 MHz = 36, plus 1 = 37 |
| 400 kHz | Fast, F/S = 1, DUTY = 0 | 36e6 / (3 × 400e3) = 30 | 0x801E | 0.833 µs / 1.667 µs | 300 ns × 36 MHz = 10.8, integer part 10, plus 1 = 11 |

To use it, pick the row for your target speed; the CCR field sets the frequency, and the F/S and DUTY bits sit on top of it to make the whole I2C_CCR. Both rows' I2C_CCR values match the 0x00B4 and 0x801E that DS5319 Rev 20 Table 42 prints for 36 MHz. Note 2 of Table 42 gives the tolerance of the actual speed: ±5% near 200 kHz and ±2% at the other speeds, depending on the accuracy of the external components.

Fast mode has two more details:

- Note 2 of Table 41 says that to reach the 400 kHz maximum, PCLK1 must be a multiple of 10 MHz. 36 MHz is not. With DUTY = 1 one period is 25 × CCR × TPCLK1, so at 36 MHz you would need CCR = 3.6 for 400 kHz; CCR must be a whole number, and 4 gives only 360 kHz. With DUTY = 0, CCR = 30 divides exactly, and the 0x801E in Table 42 is that setting.
- RM0008 section 26.6.8 notes that Thigh includes the SCL rise time (thigh = tr(SCL) + tw(SCLH)). At 400 kHz with DUTY = 0, Thigh is only 833 ns; if the rise uses 300 ns, the remaining 533 ns is already shorter than the 0.6 µs Table 41 requires. So at 400 kHz keep the rise time well under 300 ns, and do not pick the pull-up right at the upper limit from section 1.

TRISE is used for clock-stretching detection: after the peripheral releases SCL, it waits TRISE PCLK1 periods and then checks whether SCL has actually risen. If the pull-up is weak and the rise is slower than that, the peripheral treats it as a slave stretching, stops and waits, and the SCL period grows (section 26.3.3).

With HAL, `HAL_I2C_Init()` computes these three values from `ClockSpeed` and `DutyCycle`, and you can see the results in the debugger (lab 3).

### 7. Bit-banged versus hardware I2C

| Comparison | Bit-banged (toggling GPIO) | Hardware I2C (the I2C1 peripheral) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pins | Any two GPIOs | Fixed: PB6/PB7, or remapped to PB8/PB9 |
| CPU load | The CPU toggles every bit and is fully busy during the transfer | The peripheral generates the timing; the CPU only handles events |
| Speed | Set by the delays, typically around 100 kHz (rule of thumb) | Standard mode 100 kHz, fast mode 400 kHz |
| Where it goes wrong | The delays change with compiler optimization; measure with a logic analyzer | When receiving as master, the moment to clear ACK and request STOP is tight, and a delayed interrupt causes errors |

Read the table by what you are short of: if pins are taken, or you need several I2C buses, bit-bang; to save CPU time or run 400 kHz, use the hardware. When the F1's hardware I2C receives as master, it has to clear the ACK bit and request STOP before the acknowledge pulse of the last byte; when interrupt priority is not high enough, the manual gives a separate procedure (Method 2 in RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.3.3). HAL already follows these steps; you only need to follow them one by one when you write a register-level driver yourself.

### 8. A stuck bus and how to recover it

The most common case: the master is reading from a slave, the slave is driving out a 0, and the master resets at that moment. The slave is still waiting for clocks to finish the remaining bits, so it keeps SDA low. I2C1 sees SDA low and sets the BUSY bit in SR2 (BUSY is set when SDA or SCL is low and cleared only when a STOP is detected, section 26.6.7). The peripheral generates START only after BUSY clears (section 26.3.3), so START never goes out and HAL returns `HAL_BUSY`.

Recovery takes three steps:

1. Switch PB6 and PB7 to plain GPIO open-drain outputs and clock SCL one beat at a time, checking SDA after each beat. The slave has at most 8 data bits and 1 acknowledge bit left, so within 9 beats it releases SDA. The "bus clear" section of the I2C specification UM10204 gives exactly 9 clocks.
2. Once SDA is high again, make a STOP: pull SDA low while SCL is low, release SCL, then release SDA.
3. The I2C peripheral's own BUSY bit may still be locked. RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.6.1 explains that when BUSY is locked by a glitch on the bus, the SWRST bit in CR1 can reset the peripheral, and that both lines must be released before doing so. Initialize again after the reset.

If SDA is still low after 9 beats, the slave is broken or something else is holding the line, and the only fix is to power-cycle the slave.

## Hardware and wiring

| Board | Peripheral | Pin | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BluePill | I2C1_SCL | PB6 (LQFP48 pin 42) | Alternate-function open-drain; 4.7 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V |
| BluePill | I2C1_SDA | PB7 (pin 43) | Alternate-function open-drain; 4.7 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V |
| SSD1306 OLED module | 7-bit address 0x3C | Same SCL and SDA | Many modules already have pull-ups on board; check with a multimeter first (rule of thumb) |
| AT24C02 EEPROM | 7-bit address 0x50 | Same | A2, A1, A0 and the write-protect pin WP to ground |
| MPU6050 module | 7-bit address 0x68 | Same | AD0 to ground; tied high it becomes 0x69 |

Pin numbers come from DS5319 Rev 20 Table 5, and the alternate-function open-drain setting from RM0008 Rev 21 Table 27.

```text
                 3.3V ──┬────────────┬──────────────
                      [4.7k]       [4.7k]
                        │            │
BluePill PB6 (pin 42) ──┴──── SCL ───┼──── SCL of OLED / EEPROM / MPU6050
BluePill PB7 (pin 43) ──────── SDA ──┴──── SDA of OLED / EEPROM / MPU6050
BluePill GND ─────────────────────────────── GND of each module
```

Watch out when wiring: one bus needs only one set of pull-ups. If every module carries its own 4.7 kΩ, the resistors end up in parallel; by the fifth module you are down to 4.7 kΩ ÷ 5 = 940 Ω, below the 967 Ω lower limit from section 1, so remove the pull-ups on the extra modules. The pull-ups go to 3.3 V.

## Code

The first program scans the bus with hardware I2C1, then initializes the OLED and fills it with horizontal stripes. It uses no UART; the results go into global variables that you inspect with the debugger.

```c
/* i2c_scan_oled.c: scan the bus with I2C1 at 400 kHz and light an SSD1306 (HAL, STM32F103C8T6) */
#include <string.h>
#include "stm32f1xx_hal.h"

#define I2C_SPEED_HZ      400000U       /* with PCLK1 = 36 MHz HAL writes CCR = 0x801E */
#define I2C_TIMEOUT_MS    20U
#define SCAN_FIRST        0x08U         /* 0x00–0x07 and 0x78–0x7F are reserved */
#define SCAN_LAST         0x77U
#define SCAN_TIMEOUT_MS   2U
#define MAX_FOUND         8U

#define OLED_ADDR         (0x3CU << 1)  /* 7-bit 0x3C → HAL DevAddress 0x78 */
#define OLED_CTRL_CMD     0x00U         /* control byte: commands follow */
#define OLED_CTRL_DATA    0x40U         /* control byte: display data follows */
#define OLED_WIDTH        128U
#define OLED_PAGES        8U            /* 64 rows, 8 rows per page */
#define OLED_PATTERN      0x0FU         /* top 4 rows of each page on, bottom 4 off: stripes */

I2C_HandleTypeDef hi2c1;

volatile uint8_t  g_found[MAX_FOUND];   /* 7-bit addresses found by the scan */
volatile uint8_t  g_found_count;
volatile HAL_StatusTypeDef g_oled_status = HAL_ERROR;
volatile uint32_t g_i2c_error;          /* HAL_I2C_GetError() after the last OLED transfer */

/* A common init sequence for 128×64 modules, using the module's charge pump */
static const uint8_t oled_init_cmds[] = {
    0xAE,           /* display off */
    0xD5, 0x80,     /* clock divide */
    0xA8, 0x3F,     /* multiplex ratio: 64 rows */
    0xD3, 0x00,     /* display offset 0 */
    0x40,           /* start line 0 */
    0x8D, 0x14,     /* charge pump on */
    0x20, 0x00,     /* horizontal addressing mode */
    0xA1,           /* segment remap: not mirrored left to right */
    0xC8,           /* COM scan direction: not flipped top to bottom */
    0xDA, 0x12,     /* COM pin configuration */
    0x81, 0xCF,     /* contrast */
    0xD9, 0xF1,     /* precharge period */
    0xDB, 0x40,     /* VCOMH level */
    0xA4,           /* display follows RAM */
    0xA6,           /* normal, not inverted */
    0xAF            /* display on */
};

void SystemClock_Config(void);
void error_handler(void);
void i2c1_init(void);
static void i2c_scan(void);
static HAL_StatusTypeDef oled_cmds(const uint8_t *cmds, uint16_t len);
static HAL_StatusTypeDef oled_fill(uint8_t pattern);

int main(void)
{
    HAL_StatusTypeDef st;

    HAL_Init();
    SystemClock_Config();                   /* PCLK1 = 36 MHz, so CCR comes out as 0x801E */
    i2c1_init();

    i2c_scan();                             /* results in g_found[] */

    st = oled_cmds(oled_init_cmds, sizeof(oled_init_cmds));
    if (st == HAL_OK)
    {
        st = oled_fill(OLED_PATTERN);
    }
    g_oled_status = st;                     /* HAL_OK, HAL_ERROR or HAL_BUSY */
    g_i2c_error   = HAL_I2C_GetError(&hi2c1);

    while (1)
    {
    }
}

void i2c1_init(void)
{
    GPIO_InitTypeDef io = {0};

    __HAL_RCC_GPIOB_CLK_ENABLE();
    io.Pin   = GPIO_PIN_6 | GPIO_PIN_7;     /* PB6 = SCL, PB7 = SDA */
    io.Mode  = GPIO_MODE_AF_OD;             /* AF open-drain: the peripheral only pulls low; high comes from the external pull-up */
    io.Speed = GPIO_SPEED_FREQ_HIGH;
    HAL_GPIO_Init(GPIOB, &io);

    __HAL_RCC_I2C1_CLK_ENABLE();
    hi2c1.Instance             = I2C1;
    hi2c1.Init.ClockSpeed      = I2C_SPEED_HZ;
    hi2c1.Init.DutyCycle       = I2C_DUTYCYCLE_2;      /* fast mode Tlow / Thigh = 2 */
    hi2c1.Init.OwnAddress1     = 0;
    hi2c1.Init.AddressingMode  = I2C_ADDRESSINGMODE_7BIT;
    hi2c1.Init.DualAddressMode = I2C_DUALADDRESS_DISABLE;
    hi2c1.Init.OwnAddress2     = 0;
    hi2c1.Init.GeneralCallMode = I2C_GENERALCALL_DISABLE;
    hi2c1.Init.NoStretchMode   = I2C_NOSTRETCH_DISABLE;
    if (HAL_I2C_Init(&hi2c1) != HAL_OK)
    {
        error_handler();
    }
}

/* For each 7-bit address send START and address + write, and see whether the 9th clock is ACK */
static void i2c_scan(void)
{
    g_found_count = 0U;
    for (uint8_t addr7 = SCAN_FIRST; addr7 <= SCAN_LAST; addr7++)
    {
        if (HAL_I2C_IsDeviceReady(&hi2c1, (uint16_t)(addr7 << 1), 1, SCAN_TIMEOUT_MS) == HAL_OK)
        {
            if (g_found_count < MAX_FOUND)
            {
                g_found[g_found_count] = addr7;
            }
            g_found_count++;
        }
    }
}

/* One transfer: address 0x78, control byte 0x00, a run of commands */
static HAL_StatusTypeDef oled_cmds(const uint8_t *cmds, uint16_t len)
{
    return HAL_I2C_Mem_Write(&hi2c1, OLED_ADDR, OLED_CTRL_CMD, I2C_MEMADD_SIZE_8BIT,
                             (uint8_t *)cmds, len, I2C_TIMEOUT_MS);
}

/* Fill all 128 columns × 8 pages of display RAM with the same byte */
static HAL_StatusTypeDef oled_fill(uint8_t pattern)
{
    static const uint8_t window[] = {
        0x21, 0x00, OLED_WIDTH - 1U,        /* columns 0–127 */
        0x22, 0x00, OLED_PAGES - 1U         /* pages 0–7 */
    };
    uint8_t row[OLED_WIDTH];
    HAL_StatusTypeDef st;

    memset(row, pattern, sizeof(row));
    st = oled_cmds(window, sizeof(window));
    for (uint32_t page = 0; (page < OLED_PAGES) && (st == HAL_OK); page++)
    {
        st = HAL_I2C_Mem_Write(&hi2c1, OLED_ADDR, OLED_CTRL_DATA, I2C_MEMADD_SIZE_8BIT,
                               row, sizeof(row), I2C_TIMEOUT_MS);
    }
    return st;
}

    osc.OscillatorType = RCC_OSCILLATORTYPE_HSE;
    osc.HSEState       = RCC_HSE_ON;
    osc.HSEPredivValue = RCC_HSE_PREDIV_DIV1;

        error_handler();
    }

    clk.APB2CLKDivider = RCC_HCLK_DIV1;     /* PCLK2 = 72 MHz: GPIOB */

        error_handler();
    }
}

    }
```

Piece by piece:

- `i2c1_init()` first configures PB6 and PB7 as alternate-function open-drain, then enables the I2C1 clock and calls `HAL_I2C_Init()`. `HAL_I2C_Init()` only calls the weakly defined `HAL_I2C_MspInit()`; if you neither define that nor configure the pins yourself as here, the two pins stay in their reset state, floating input, and the peripheral never reaches the bus.
- With `ClockSpeed = 400000` and `DutyCycle = I2C_DUTYCYCLE_2`, the I2C_CCR that HAL computes for PCLK1 = 36 MHz is 0x801E and TRISE is 11 (section 6). Change it to 100000 and you get 0x00B4 and 37.
- `HAL_I2C_IsDeviceReady()` sends START and the address with write, checks for an ACK on the 9th clock, and sends STOP. The scan runs from 0x08 to 0x77 and skips the reserved addresses.
- In every transfer the SSD1306 receives, the first data byte is a control byte: 0x00 means everything after it is commands, 0x40 means everything after it is display data. `HAL_I2C_Mem_Write()` sends START, the device address, one "memory address" byte, the data, and STOP; passing the control byte as the memory address gives exactly the format the SSD1306 wants, with no extra buffer copy.
- The `0x8D, 0x14` in the init sequence turns on the charge pump. Most 0.96-inch modules use it to make the high voltage the panel needs; without those two bytes every command is accepted but the screen stays dark. What each command does is in the SSD1306 datasheet (not in this book's source list); with a different module, check its own datasheet.
- One page is 128 bytes plus the address and control bytes: 130 bytes of 9 clocks each, about 2.9 ms at 400 kHz. A full screen of 8 pages takes about 23 ms.
- When a transfer fails, look at two variables: if `g_oled_status` is `HAL_BUSY`, the bus is most likely held low (no pull-ups, or a stuck slave, section 8); if it is `HAL_ERROR` and `g_i2c_error` includes `HAL_I2C_ERROR_AF`, an address or data byte got no ACK. This mapping of return values to error codes is how the STM32CubeF1 HAL source is written; check the source when you change HAL versions.

The second piece of code reads and writes an AT24C02 EEPROM. It lives in another file of the same project and uses `hi2c1` from the first program.

```c
/* at24c02.c: AT24C02 page-split writes, acknowledge polling, random reads (HAL) */
#include "stm32f1xx_hal.h"

#define AT24_ADDR        (0x50U << 1)   /* A2, A1, A0 grounded: 7-bit 0x50 → DevAddress 0xA0 */
#define AT24_SIZE        256U           /* 2 Kbit = 256 bytes */
#define AT24_PAGE        8U             /* 8 bytes per page */
#define AT24_POLL_MS     10U            /* write cycle at most 5 ms; allow twice that */
#define AT24_TIMEOUT_MS  20U

extern I2C_HandleTypeDef hi2c1;

/* During its internal write cycle the EEPROM does not acknowledge its address; an ACK means the write is done */
static HAL_StatusTypeDef at24_wait_ready(void)
{
    uint32_t t0 = HAL_GetTick();

    while (HAL_I2C_IsDeviceReady(&hi2c1, AT24_ADDR, 1, 1) != HAL_OK)
    {
        if ((HAL_GetTick() - t0) > AT24_POLL_MS)
        {
            return HAL_TIMEOUT;
        }
    }
    return HAL_OK;
}

/* Write len bytes from mem. One write must not cross a page, or the in-page address wraps and overwrites the start of the page */
HAL_StatusTypeDef at24_write(uint16_t mem, const uint8_t *data, uint16_t len)
{
    if ((mem + len) > AT24_SIZE)
    {
        return HAL_ERROR;
    }
    while (len > 0U)
    {
        uint16_t room = (uint16_t)(AT24_PAGE - (mem % AT24_PAGE));
        uint16_t n    = (len < room) ? len : room;
        HAL_StatusTypeDef st = HAL_I2C_Mem_Write(&hi2c1, AT24_ADDR, mem, I2C_MEMADD_SIZE_8BIT,
                                                 (uint8_t *)data, n, AT24_TIMEOUT_MS);
        if (st == HAL_OK)
        {
            st = at24_wait_ready();
        }
        if (st != HAL_OK)
        {
            return st;
        }
        mem  = (uint16_t)(mem + n);
        data += n;
        len  = (uint16_t)(len - n);
    }
    return HAL_OK;
}

/* Random read: write the word address, repeated start, read len bytes in a row */
HAL_StatusTypeDef at24_read(uint16_t mem, uint8_t *data, uint16_t len)
{
    if ((mem + len) > AT24_SIZE)
    {
        return HAL_ERROR;
    }
    return HAL_I2C_Mem_Read(&hi2c1, AT24_ADDR, mem, I2C_MEMADD_SIZE_8BIT, data, len, AT24_TIMEOUT_MS);
}
```

Piece by piece:

- The AT24C02's 256-byte size, 8-byte page and 5 ms maximum write cycle all come from the AT24C02 datasheet (not in this book's source list). Page size differs between vendors and densities; change `AT24_PAGE` when you change the part.
- One page write can only write within one page. Once the address reaches the end of the page, the device's internal address wraps within that page and a 9th byte overwrites the page's 1st byte, so `at24_write()` splits at page boundaries. Writing 16 bytes from 0x06 splits into three writes of 2, 8 and 6 bytes.
- After writing a page, the EEPROM spends a few milliseconds actually storing the data and does not acknowledge its address meanwhile. `at24_wait_ready()` keeps sending the address, and an ACK means the write is done; this is faster than a fixed `HAL_Delay(5)` and never waits too little.
- `at24_read()` uses `HAL_I2C_Mem_Read()`, so the wire shows the "write word address, repeated start, read" order from section 4.

For an MPU6050 the same two functions are enough; only the register addresses change, as below. The register addresses and values come from the MPU-6050 register map, which is not in this book's source list:

| Step | Register | Write or expect | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Check identity | WHO_AM_I (0x75) | Reads 0x68 | Reading it does not need a wake-up first |
| Wake up | PWR_MGMT_1 (0x6B) | Write 0x00 | It powers up asleep (reset value 0x40); until the SLEEP bit is cleared, the measurement registers do not update |
| Sample rate | SMPLRT_DIV (0x19) | As needed | Sample rate divider |
| Gyro range | GYRO_CONFIG (0x1B) | 0x18 | ±2000 °/s |
| Accel range | ACCEL_CONFIG (0x1C) | 0x00 | ±2 g |

Working down the table once gives a minimal initialization. To read the 6 acceleration bytes, start at ACCEL_XOUT_H (0x3B) and read them in one `HAL_I2C_Mem_Read()`; the device's internal register address increments by itself.

The third piece of code recovers a stuck bus, following the three steps in section 8.

```c
/* i2c_recover.c: when a slave holds SDA, clock SCL up to 9 times, make a STOP, then reset I2C1 */
#include "stm32f1xx_hal.h"

#define I2C_PORT          GPIOB
#define I2C_SCL_PIN       GPIO_PIN_6
#define I2C_SDA_PIN       GPIO_PIN_7
#define RECOVER_CLOCKS    9U
#define RECOVER_HALF_MS   1U            /* half period 1 ms: slow, but I2C has no minimum speed */

extern I2C_HandleTypeDef hi2c1;
void i2c1_init(void);

HAL_StatusTypeDef i2c_bus_recover(void)
{
    GPIO_InitTypeDef io = {0};

    HAL_I2C_DeInit(&hi2c1);                                 /* turn the peripheral off first */

    HAL_GPIO_WritePin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SCL_PIN | I2C_SDA_PIN, GPIO_PIN_SET);   /* write 1 first: released */
    io.Pin   = I2C_SCL_PIN | I2C_SDA_PIN;
    io.Mode  = GPIO_MODE_OUTPUT_OD;                         /* open-drain output; IDR shows the line level */
    io.Speed = GPIO_SPEED_FREQ_LOW;
    HAL_GPIO_Init(I2C_PORT, &io);

    for (uint32_t i = 0; i < RECOVER_CLOCKS; i++)
    {
        if (HAL_GPIO_ReadPin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SDA_PIN) == GPIO_PIN_SET)
        {
            break;                                          /* the slave has released SDA */
        }
        HAL_GPIO_WritePin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SCL_PIN, GPIO_PIN_RESET);
        HAL_Delay(RECOVER_HALF_MS);
        HAL_GPIO_WritePin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SCL_PIN, GPIO_PIN_SET);
        HAL_Delay(RECOVER_HALF_MS);
    }

    /* STOP: pull SDA low while SCL is low, release SCL, then release SDA while SCL is high */
    HAL_GPIO_WritePin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SCL_PIN, GPIO_PIN_RESET);
    HAL_Delay(RECOVER_HALF_MS);
    HAL_GPIO_WritePin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SDA_PIN, GPIO_PIN_RESET);
    HAL_Delay(RECOVER_HALF_MS);
    HAL_GPIO_WritePin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SCL_PIN, GPIO_PIN_SET);
    HAL_Delay(RECOVER_HALF_MS);
    HAL_GPIO_WritePin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SDA_PIN, GPIO_PIN_SET);
    HAL_Delay(RECOVER_HALF_MS);

    if ((HAL_GPIO_ReadPin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SDA_PIN) == GPIO_PIN_RESET) ||
        (HAL_GPIO_ReadPin(I2C_PORT, I2C_SCL_PIN) == GPIO_PIN_RESET))
    {
        return HAL_ERROR;                                   /* still held: power-cycle the slave */
    }

    /* Both lines are released; now reset the peripheral, whose BUSY bit may still be locked */
    __HAL_RCC_I2C1_CLK_ENABLE();
    SET_BIT(I2C1->CR1, I2C_CR1_SWRST);
    CLEAR_BIT(I2C1->CR1, I2C_CR1_SWRST);
    i2c1_init();                                            /* pins back to AF open-drain, speed set again */
    return HAL_OK;
}
```

Piece by piece:

- Writing 1 before switching the pins to output means the pin is already released at the moment of the switch and does not pull the bus low an extra time.
- In the F1's open-drain output mode, reading the input data register IDR gives the real level on the pin (RM0008 Rev 21 section 9.1.8), so the same pin can pull low as an output and read back whether a slave is holding the line.
- Before each beat it checks SDA and stops as soon as SDA is released, with at most 9 beats.
- `HAL_Delay(1)` makes the clock only about 500 Hz. I2C specifies only minimum high and low times and no minimum speed, so slow is fine.
- SWRST is set and cleared only after both lines are confirmed released, the order section 26.6.1 requires. The reset returns CR2, CCR and TRISE to their reset values, so `i2c1_init()` is called again at the end; it also puts the two pins back to alternate-function open-drain.

The fourth piece of code is bit-banged I2C. Any two GPIOs work; this one uses PB6 and PB7 again, so it is either this or hardware I2C1.

```c
/* soft_i2c.c: an I2C master on two open-drain GPIOs (SCL = PB6, SDA = PB7) */
#include "stm32f1xx_hal.h"

#define SI2C_PORT           GPIOB
#define SI2C_SCL            GPIO_PIN_6
#define SI2C_SDA            GPIO_PIN_7
#define SI2C_DELAY_LOOPS    50U         /* rule of thumb: about 100 kHz or slower at 72 MHz; measure with a logic analyzer, then tune */
#define SI2C_STRETCH_LOOPS  10000U      /* limit on waiting for a slave to release SCL */

#define SCL_L()   HAL_GPIO_WritePin(SI2C_PORT, SI2C_SCL, GPIO_PIN_RESET)
#define SDA_H()   HAL_GPIO_WritePin(SI2C_PORT, SI2C_SDA, GPIO_PIN_SET)
#define SDA_L()   HAL_GPIO_WritePin(SI2C_PORT, SI2C_SDA, GPIO_PIN_RESET)
#define SDA_IN()  (HAL_GPIO_ReadPin(SI2C_PORT, SI2C_SDA) == GPIO_PIN_SET)

static void si2c_delay(void)
{
    for (volatile uint32_t i = 0; i < SI2C_DELAY_LOOPS; i++)
    {
    }
}

/* Release SCL and wait for the slave to let go: it may be stretching the clock */
static void scl_release(void)
{
    uint32_t n = 0;

    HAL_GPIO_WritePin(SI2C_PORT, SI2C_SCL, GPIO_PIN_SET);
    while ((HAL_GPIO_ReadPin(SI2C_PORT, SI2C_SCL) == GPIO_PIN_RESET) && (n < SI2C_STRETCH_LOOPS))
    {
        n++;
    }
}

void si2c_init(void)
{
    GPIO_InitTypeDef io = {0};

    __HAL_RCC_GPIOB_CLK_ENABLE();
    HAL_GPIO_WritePin(SI2C_PORT, SI2C_SCL | SI2C_SDA, GPIO_PIN_SET);       /* both lines released */
    io.Pin   = SI2C_SCL | SI2C_SDA;
    io.Mode  = GPIO_MODE_OUTPUT_OD;     /* open drain: only pulls low; high comes from the external pull-up */
    io.Speed = GPIO_SPEED_FREQ_HIGH;
    HAL_GPIO_Init(SI2C_PORT, &io);
}

void si2c_start(void)
{
    SDA_H();
    scl_release();
    si2c_delay();
    SDA_L();                            /* SDA falls while SCL is high: START */
    si2c_delay();
    SCL_L();
    si2c_delay();
}

void si2c_stop(void)
{
    SDA_L();
    si2c_delay();
    scl_release();
    si2c_delay();
    SDA_H();                            /* SDA rises while SCL is high: STOP */
    si2c_delay();
}

/* Send one byte MSB first; returns 1 if the receiver pulled SDA low on the 9th clock (ACK) */
int si2c_write(uint8_t byte)
{
    int ack;

    for (int i = 7; i >= 0; i--)
    {
        if ((byte & (1U << i)) != 0U)
        {
            SDA_H();
        }
        else
        {
            SDA_L();
        }
        si2c_delay();                   /* change SDA while SCL is low */
        scl_release();
        si2c_delay();                   /* the receiver reads while SCL is high */
        SCL_L();
    }
    SDA_H();                            /* hand SDA to the receiver for the 9th clock */
    si2c_delay();
    scl_release();
    si2c_delay();
    ack = SDA_IN() ? 0 : 1;             /* low is ACK */
    SCL_L();
    si2c_delay();
    return ack;
}

/* Read one byte MSB first; ack = 1 answers ACK, pass 0 on the last byte to answer NACK */
uint8_t si2c_read(int ack)
{
    uint8_t byte = 0;

    SDA_H();                            /* release SDA so the slave can drive it */
    for (int i = 7; i >= 0; i--)
    {
        si2c_delay();
        scl_release();
        si2c_delay();
        if (SDA_IN())
        {
            byte |= (uint8_t)(1U << i);
        }
        SCL_L();
    }
    if (ack != 0)
    {
        SDA_L();
    }
    else
    {
        SDA_H();
    }
    si2c_delay();
    scl_release();
    si2c_delay();
    SCL_L();
    SDA_H();
    return byte;
}

/* Does a device answer at 7-bit address addr7? */
int si2c_probe(uint8_t addr7)
{
    int ack;

    si2c_start();
    ack = si2c_write((uint8_t)(addr7 << 1));    /* write direction */
    si2c_stop();
    return ack;
}
```

Piece by piece:

- Both pins are open-drain outputs. Writing 1 only "lets go"; whether the line is high depends on whether anyone else is pulling, so after sending a 1 you read IDR back to know. Reading the ACK and reading data both rely on this.
- `scl_release()` releases SCL and waits until it really goes high. When a slave stretches the clock it holds SCL down for a while; the wait has a limit, so a line stuck low does not hang the program here forever.
- `SI2C_DELAY_LOOPS` sets the speed. One pass of the empty loop takes a few clock cycles and varies with the compiler's optimization level, so 50 is only a rule of thumb. Measure SCL with a logic analyzer and settle on a value once the low time is at least 4.7 µs and the high time at least 4.0 µs (standard mode, Table 41).
- Pass `ack = 0` when reading the last byte: the master answers NACK and the slave stops sending (section 2).
- If `si2c_probe(0x3C)` returns 1, the OLED is on the bus; this should agree with the hardware scan.

Register version: the same I2C1 at 400 kHz, to see which registers HAL writes for you, then one "control byte 0x00 plus command 0xAE" sent by hand.

```c
/* Register version: I2C1 at 400 kHz (PCLK1 = 36 MHz), write a control byte and one command to 0x3C. Teaching only, no timeouts */
RCC->APB2ENR |= RCC_APB2ENR_IOPBEN;
GPIOB->CRL = (GPIOB->CRL & ~(0xFFUL << 24)) | (0xFFUL << 24);  /* PB6, PB7: AF open-drain 50 MHz (CNF = 11, MODE = 11 → 0xF) */
RCC->APB1ENR |= RCC_APB1ENR_I2C1EN;

I2C1->CR1   = I2C_CR1_SWRST;            /* reset the peripheral */
I2C1->CR1   = 0;                        /* CCR and TRISE can only be written with PE = 0 */
I2C1->CR2   = 36;                       /* FREQ = PCLK1 in MHz */
I2C1->CCR   = 0x801E;                   /* F/S = 1, DUTY = 0, CCR = 30 */
I2C1->TRISE = 11;                       /* 300 ns × 36 MHz = 10.8 → 10 + 1 */
I2C1->CR1   = I2C_CR1_PE;

I2C1->CR1 |= I2C_CR1_START;
while ((I2C1->SR1 & I2C_SR1_SB) == 0) { }                   /* START has gone out */
I2C1->DR = 0x78;                                            /* SR1 was read, now write DR: clears SB */
while ((I2C1->SR1 & (I2C_SR1_ADDR | I2C_SR1_AF)) == 0) { }
if ((I2C1->SR1 & I2C_SR1_AF) != 0)                          /* the 9th clock was NACK */
{
    I2C1->CR1 |= I2C_CR1_STOP;
    I2C1->SR1 = ~I2C_SR1_AF;                                /* AF clears on writing 0; bits written with 1 are unaffected */
}
else
{
    (void)I2C1->SR2;                                        /* SR1 was read, now read SR2: clears ADDR */
    I2C1->DR = 0x00;                                        /* control byte */
    while ((I2C1->SR1 & I2C_SR1_TXE) == 0) { }
    I2C1->DR = 0xAE;                                        /* command: display off */
    while ((I2C1->SR1 & I2C_SR1_BTF) == 0) { }              /* the last byte and its acknowledge are done */
    I2C1->CR1 |= I2C_CR1_STOP;
}
```

`CRL` covers PB0 to PB7 with 4 bits per pin; PB6 is bits 27:24 and PB7 is bits 31:28. How each flag is cleared is in the transfer sequence diagrams of RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.3.3: SB by reading SR1 and then writing DR, ADDR by reading SR1 and then reading SR2, the same idea as chapter 06's "read SR, then DR" for the USART; AF is cleared by software writing 0 (section 26.6.6). After a NACK the master must send a STOP or a repeated start itself (section 26.3.4).

The full project is in [`examples/i2c-oled/`](../../examples/i2c-oled/). It runs at 100 kHz with a font and full-screen refresh, but it does not define `HAL_I2C_MspInit()` and does not configure PB6 and PB7 as alternate-function open-drain anywhere else; its comment says `HAL_I2C_Init` does that for you, and it does not. Add the pin-setup lines the way `i2c1_init()` in this section does before you use it.

## Hands-on labs

1. **Lab 1: scan the bus.** Connect only the OLED module and load the first program.
   - Expected: horizontal stripes on the screen; in the debugger `g_found_count` is 1, `g_found[0]` is 0x3C and `g_oled_status` is `HAL_OK`.
   - How to check: put a logic analyzer on PB6 and PB7 with I2C decoding. During the scan most addresses are followed by NACK; only the write byte 0x78 is followed by ACK. One SCL period measures about 2.5 µs. Remove the pull-up resistors (not possible if the module has its own) and run again: the scan should find nothing.
   - If it fails, check first: are the pull-ups fitted and tied to 3.3 V; are SCL and SDA swapped; are PB6 and PB7 alternate-function open-drain; if `g_oled_status` is `HAL_BUSY`, measure whether both lines idle high.
2. **Lab 2: EEPROM write, read, and retention without power.** Add the AT24C02 and `at24c02.c`; in `main()`, after the scan, write 16 bytes starting at address 0x06 and read them back to compare. Then power off, load a program that only reads, and read again.
   - Expected: both read-backs match the 16 bytes written, and the scan results include 0x50.
   - How to check: on the logic analyzer the 16 bytes go out as three page writes of 2, 8 and 6 bytes; each page write is followed by a run of 0xA0 bytes that get no ACK, lasting no more than 5 ms; that is acknowledge polling. The read shows 0xA0, 0x06, a repeated start and 0xA1, with no STOP in between.
   - If it fails, check first: how A2, A1 and A0 are tied (the address may be another of 0x50–0x57); whether WP is grounded; whether `at24_write()` returns `HAL_OK`.
3. **Lab 3: check CCR and TRISE.** In the lab 1 program, set a breakpoint after `HAL_I2C_Init()` and look at the I2C1 registers in the debugger.
   - Expected: the FREQ field of CR2 is 36 (0x24), CCR is 0x801E, and TRISE is 11 (0x0B). Change `I2C_SPEED_HZ` to 100000 and rebuild: CCR becomes 0x00B4 and TRISE 37 (0x25).
   - How to check: measure SCL with a logic analyzer: at 400 kHz the low time is about 1.67 µs and the high time about 0.83 µs; at 100 kHz each is about 5 µs. Compare with the table in section 6.
   - If it fails, check first: is PCLK1 36 MHz (is `SystemClock_Config()` called before `i2c1_init()`); if the period is clearly too long, measure whether the rise time exceeds 300 ns.

## Self-test

1. An MPU6050 has its AD0 pin tied high, so its 7-bit address becomes 0x69. What are the write byte and the read byte on the wire? What goes into HAL's DevAddress?
2. PCLK1 = 36 MHz and you want 100 kHz standard mode. What do you write to I2C_CCR and TRISE?
3. The bus capacitance is estimated at 150 pF and you want 400 kHz. What is the largest pull-up you can use? Is the 4.7 kΩ from DS5319 Table 42 suitable?
4. To read a register, code first calls `HAL_I2C_Master_Transmit` to write the register address and then `HAL_I2C_Master_Receive` to read the data. What appears on the wire between the two calls? What should be there for a register read?
5. Five modules share one bus, each with its own 4.7 kΩ pull-up. What is the total pull-up resistance? Is it acceptable at 3.3 V?

Answers:

1. 0x69 = 110 1001; shifted left by 1 it is 1101 0010 = 0xD2 (write), and with the lowest bit set it is 0xD3 (read). DevAddress takes 0xD2, that is `0x69 << 1`.
2. CCR field = 36 000 000 / (2 × 100 000) = 180 = 0xB4; standard mode has F/S = 0, so I2C_CCR = 0x00B4. TRISE = 1000 ns × 36 MHz + 1 = 36 + 1 = 37.
3. Rp(max) = 300 ns / (0.8473 × 150 pF) ≈ 2.36 kΩ. 4.7 kΩ is larger: its rise time is about 0.8473 × 4.7 kΩ × 150 pF ≈ 597 ns, more than 300 ns, so it is not suitable. Use 2.2 kΩ, which is below 2.36 kΩ and above the 967 Ω lower limit.
4. Between the two calls there is a STOP and a new START. A register read should have a repeated start Sr there, with no STOP; `HAL_I2C_Mem_Read` does it in one call.
5. Five 4.7 kΩ in parallel: 4.7 kΩ ÷ 5 = 940 Ω. That is below the lower limit of (3.3 − 0.4) V / 3 mA ≈ 967 Ω; when a device pulls low it sinks more than 3 mA, and the low level may not get below 0.4 V. Keep the pull-ups on one or two modules and remove the rest.

## Principle and scope

> [!NOTE]
> From this section to "Pitfalls and sources" is the deep part. It pins the conclusions above to one part number, one manual section, and numbers you can recompute. On a first read you can skip it and come back after the labs.

The I2C conclusions in this chapter are pinned to one order code, STM32F103C8T6.

**Pins.** The reset-default pins are pin 42 PB6 = I2C1_SCL and pin 43 PB7 = I2C1_SDA, both configured as alternate-function open-drain, each with 4.7 kΩ to 3.3 V ([DS5319 Rev 20 Tables 5 and 42](#source-ds5319-i2c)). I2C1 can be remapped to pin 45 PB8 and pin 46 PB9 by setting the I2C1_REMAP bit in AFIO_MAPR; that is not on this chapter's normal path. The remap column of Table 5 also puts USART1_TX and USART1_RX on PB6 and PB7, which is not the reset default either (chapter 06 uses PA9 and PA10); at any one time the two pins cannot be both I2C1 and USART1.

**Clock.** I2C1 is on APB1, and in this book's setup PCLK1 = 36 MHz, the same figure as the APB1 cell in Appendix D. FREQ in I2C_CR2 is 36.

**Address byte.** The bit pattern of 7-bit `0x3C` is `0111100`. The first byte on the wire for a write is `0x3C << 1`, which is `0x78`; for a read it is `0x79`. HAL's DevAddress is `0x78`. Passing the scanner's `0x3C` straight into `HAL_I2C_Master_Transmit` puts `0x3C` on the wire, and the 7-bit address becomes `0x1E`; that is the failure path in the main figure. The example macro is already `0x78`; see [examples/i2c-oled](#source-project-examples). How the address byte is built: [RM0008 Rev 21 chapter 26](#source-rm0008-i2c).

**Acknowledge.** On the 9th clock, the receiver pulling SDA low is ACK and SDA staying high is NACK. A wrong address shows NACK; a high level does not mean "yes". When the master receives a NACK, the AF bit in SR1 is set and software must send a STOP or a repeated start itself (RM0008 Rev 21 section 26.3.4).

**Reading a register.** `HAL_I2C_Mem_Read` sends START, device address with write, register address, repeated start, device address with read, data, STOP. `Master_Transmit` followed by `Master_Receive` puts a STOP in the middle.

**The rate cell.** `0x801E` breaks down as: fast-mode bit F/S set, DUTY = 0, CCR field = 30. With PCLK1 = 36 MHz one SCL period is `3 × 30 / 36e6 = 2.5` µs, a 400 kHz target. The 100 kHz row is `0x00B4`, CCR field = 180, standard mode, `36e6 / (2 × 180) = 100` kHz. The CCR formulas and the "write only with PE = 0" rule come from [RM0008 Rev 21](#source-rm0008) section 26.6.8. Note 2 of Table 41 says that reaching the 400 kHz maximum needs PCLK1 to be a multiple of 10 MHz, which 36 MHz is not; Table 42 still prints 400 kHz and `0x801E` in the 36 MHz column. Keep the ±2% tolerance from note 2 of Table 42, and do not write 400 kHz up as a guaranteed edge.

**1 MHz.** RM0008 section 26.1 lists only standard mode (100 kHz) and fast mode (400 kHz), and Tables 41 and 42 have no 1 MHz entry. Fast-mode Plus at 1 MHz is void on STM32F103C8T6 and is not one of this chapter's working points.

**Rise time and pull-up.** Table 41 sets a rise-time limit of 1000 ns in standard mode and 300 ns in fast mode, with at most 400 pF of bus capacitance per line; the Table 42 row uses a 4.7 kΩ external pull-up. By the RC curve, 4.7 kΩ stays within 300 ns up to about 75 pF. In alternate-function open-drain mode the on-chip pull-up and pull-down are disconnected (RM0008 Rev 21 section 9.1.9), and the on-chip pull-up is 30–50 kΩ anyway (DS5319 Rev 20 Table 36), so it cannot replace this external resistor, let alone run 400 kHz.

**Not true open drain.** DS5319 section 5.3.16 says the pins carrying SDA and SCL are not "true" open-drain: in open-drain configuration the PMOS to VDD is switched off, but the transistor is still there. The external pull-up is still required, and it still goes to 3.3 V.

## Timing and state

The table walks once through "write address, check acknowledge, then read" in time order; the last rows are the rate and pull-up working points. Durations assume 400 kHz with a 2.5 µs SCL period. The address byte and acknowledge come from [RM0008 Rev 21 chapter 26](#source-rm0008-i2c), and the rate and rise time from [DS5319 Rev 20 Tables 41 and 42](#source-ds5319-i2c).

| Segment | SCL | SDA | Byte on the wire | Duration at 400 kHz |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| START | High | High to low | No address byte yet | Held at least 0.6 µs |
| Normal: write address | 8 clocks | Shifted out, MSB first | `0x78` | 8 × 2.5 = 20 µs |
| Normal: 9th clock | 9th clock | Low | ACK | 2.5 µs |
| Failure: write address | 8 clocks | Shifted out | `0x3C`, which is 7-bit address `0x1E` | 20 µs |
| Failure: 9th clock | 9th clock | High | NACK; AF is set and the master sends STOP | 2.5 µs |
| Read direction | After the repeated start | Address byte | `0x79`, with no STOP in between | 22.5 µs including the acknowledge |
| Rate | `0x801E` targets 400 kHz | `0x00B4` is 100 kHz | CCR fields 30 and 180, tolerance ±2% | Periods 2.5 µs and 10 µs |
| Rise time | Fast mode at most 300 ns | Standard mode at most 1000 ns | TRISE 11 and 37 | 4.7 kΩ drives up to about 75 pF |
| Pull-up | External 4.7 kΩ to 3.3 V | 36 MHz, 3.3 V | On-chip pull-ups alone do not run this 400 kHz row | — |

Rise time: 1000 ns in standard mode, 300 ns in fast mode. 1 MHz is void on this part and is not a working point.

## Pitfalls and sources

| What you see | Why | What to do | Source | What the source supports |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A scanner shows `0x3C` and HAL gets no ACK | DevAddress was the 7-bit value | Write DevAddress as `0x78`; the read byte on the wire is `0x79` | [RM0008](#source-rm0008-i2c) | Shift the 7-bit address left by 1 and add the R/W bit; sending `0x3C` unchanged addresses `0x1E` |
| SDA is high on the 9th clock after the address | No device acknowledged: wrong address, device unpowered or not connected | Check the address and the wiring first; after a NACK the master sends STOP | [RM0008](#source-rm0008-i2c) | SDA pulled low on the 9th clock is ACK; SDA left high is NACK |
| The example and the manual disagree on the address form | 7-bit and 8-bit forms were mixed | The example macro is already `0x78` | [Examples](#source-project-examples) | The device address macro in `examples/i2c-oled` is `0x78`, from `0x3C << 1` |
| No external pull-ups, counting on the on-chip ones | In alternate-function mode the on-chip pull-up and pull-down are both disconnected | Fit external pull-up resistors | [RM0008](#source-rm0008) | Section 9.1.9: in alternate-function configuration the weak pull-up and pull-down are disconnected |
| Bit-banging with the pins as input pull-up, SCL edges are rounded and the speed will not go up | The on-chip pull-up is about 30–50 kΩ, so the rise is too slow | Add the Table 42 4.7 kΩ to 3.3 V, and a smaller value for long wiring | [DS5319](#source-ds5319-i2c) | At 36 MHz and 3.3 V the 400 kHz row uses Rp = 4.7 kΩ; the on-chip kilohms come from DS5319 Table 36 and are not set by this source |
| SCL is off from 400 kHz | CCR is not the Table 42 row, or the rise time exceeds TRISE | Check that `I2C_CCR` is `0x801E` and TRISE is 11, and allow ±2% | [DS5319](#source-ds5319-i2c) | Table 42 at 36 MHz prints 400 kHz as `0x801E`, and its note 2 gives a ±2% tolerance; the achieved rate is not guaranteed to be exactly 400 kHz |
| The waveform is on PB8/PB9, or the UART fights over PB6 | A remap is on, or USART1 is also remapped to these pins | The reset default is pin 42 and pin 43, with only I2C1 enabled | [DS5319](#source-ds5319-i2c) | Pin 42's default includes I2C1_SCL and pin 43's default includes I2C1_SDA; the USART1 remap is not the reset default |
| After the master resets mid-transfer, HAL keeps returning `HAL_BUSY` | A slave holds SDA, the BUSY bit stays 1, and START cannot go out | Clock SCL up to 9 times, make a STOP, then reset the peripheral with SWRST | [RM0008](#source-rm0008) | Section 26.6.1: when BUSY is locked by a glitch, SWRST can reset the peripheral, after both lines are released |

A few items are not in the table because no source card in the list can back them: the 9-clock bus clear and the 3 mA and 0.4 V behind the pull-up lower limit come from NXP's I2C specification UM10204; the charge-pump command `0x8D, 0x14` from the SSD1306 datasheet; the 8-byte page and 5 ms write cycle from the AT24C02 datasheet; and the mapping of `HAL_BUSY` and `HAL_I2C_ERROR_AF` from the STM32CubeF1 HAL source.
